# Academic PowerPoint Skill

Create clear and professional PowerPoint slides for academic presentations.

## Basic principles

- Use 16:9 PowerPoint format.
- Keep each slide focused on one main message.
- Prefer figures, diagrams, equations, and tables over long text.
- Keep text concise; slides support the speaker rather than replace them.
- Use 20 pt or larger for body text whenever possible; annotations may be as small as 16 pt.
- Use large fonts readable from a distance.
- Maintain consistent fonts, colors, margins, and layouts.
- Avoid decorative elements that do not communicate information.

## Academic structure

Typical research presentation:

1. Title
2. Background / Motivation
3. Problem or Research Question
4. Method
5. Results
6. Discussion
7. Conclusion
8. References / Appendix

Adjust the structure according to the presentation.

## Slide titles

Prefer titles that communicate the takeaway.

Bad:
"Experimental Results"

Better:
"Our method improves accuracy across all datasets"

## Figures and equations

- Use figures whenever they explain the idea better than text.
- Simplify figures from papers when necessary.
- Make axis labels and legends large enough for presentation.
- Do not put too many equations on one slide.
- Explain the meaning of an equation visually when possible.

## Content density

- One main idea per slide.
- Avoid paragraphs.
- Usually use 3–5 short bullet points at most.
- Move detailed derivations and supplementary results to appendix slides.

## References

Cite external figures, data, and research.
Use short citations on slides and include full references at the end.

## Final check

Before completing the presentation:

- Check that the story is logically connected.
- Check that all text is readable.
- Check that figures are not stretched or cropped.
- Check that nothing overlaps.
- Check that terminology and notation are consistent.
